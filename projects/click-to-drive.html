01 · Overview

Connecting a screen click to physical robot motion.

For this University of Michigan robotics course project, I worked on the software infrastructure connecting a laptop interface to a physical MBot. The Python GUI displays LiDAR scans and robot odometry, and lets a user click a destination to send a driving goal to the robot.

The project brings user interaction, sensor feedback, and robot control together through TCP connections and RIX messaging. My work was within a course-provided MBot platform and software framework, integrating supplied components with the project implementation.

02 · Technical approach

Building the communication path between interface and robot.

TCP socket communication

Worked on TCP socket communication, including nonblocking connections, to support communication between processes on the laptop and robot.

RIX nodes and messaging

Worked with RIX nodes, publishers, and subscribers to connect driving goals, robot pose, and LiDAR measurements through named topics.

Drive-node integration

Worked on a drive_node that receives goals, interfaces with the MBot drive system and odometry, and publishes robot pose for feedback in the GUI.

03 · Diagrams

Discovery through rixhub; topic data over direct TCP connections.

The Python GUI publishes /drive/goal and subscribes to /mbot/pose and /lidar/scan. On the MBot, drive_node subscribes to goals, controls the robot through its drive interface, and publishes pose derived from odometry. The lidar_node publishes sensor measurements on /lidar/scan.

rixhub supports node registration and discovery. Once publishers and subscribers connect, topic data travels directly between them over TCP.
